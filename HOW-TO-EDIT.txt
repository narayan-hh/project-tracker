MY WORKSTATION
===============
A local website. Nothing is online. Open index.html to start.


THE SECTIONS (left menu)
------------------------
HOME           A summary of everything - open tasks, team progress,
               upcoming reminders and total spend.
MY TASKS       Three columns: Priority 1, 2 and 3.
WEEKLY REVIEW  What moved, what slipped, who needs a conversation.
TEAM MEMBERS   A card per person, with a progress meter.
REMINDERS      Date, reminder, who, note. Overdue ones turn red.
BUDGET         Date, Expense Title, Amount, Vendor, By (M4C emp),
               Budget Head, Approved by.
SETTINGS       (bottom) Spreadsheets, backups, site details.

A red number next to Weekly Review is the count of things needing
a look right now. When it is gone, nothing is overdue or stalled.


KEYBOARD - THE FAST WAY TO WORK
-------------------------------
Ctrl+K   the jump box. Type a few letters of any task, goal, person,
         reminder or expense and press Enter to go straight there.
         It also runs actions - type "import", "csv", "reminder".
/        the same jump box
n        new item on whichever page you are on
i        import a spreadsheet into this page
g then h / t / w / m / r / b / s   go to Home, Tasks, Weekly review,
         teaM, Reminders, Budget, Settings
?        the full list of shortcuts
Esc      close whatever is open


MY TASKS
--------
The page opens with an overview, and the three priority columns
are underneath it. Scroll past the overview and the list is
exactly as it was.

Each task has a month, a name, a note, a due date and a status.
Statuses: Not started / In progress / Completed.

  - DRAG a card into another column to change its priority.
    The P1/P2/P3 dropdown still works too.
  - Due dates colour themselves: red once overdue, amber within
    three days.
  - Completed tasks drop to the bottom of their column.
  - The filter bar searches, and narrows by month, status, or
    "Overdue" only.

THE THREE COLOURS
-----------------
Every card, bar and figure on this page uses the same three:

  GREEN   Completed        the status is Completed
  AMBER   On track         still open, and not past its due date
  ROSE    Needs attention  still open, and past its due date

None of that is typed in. The status dropdown says what stage a
task is at; the due date says whether it is in trouble; the
colour is worked out from the two. Change either one and the
card, the bars and the tiles all move together.

A task with no due date counts as On track - nothing about it
says it is late. The overview counts those separately, in the
line under the meter, so they are not quietly forgotten.

The colour is never the only thing saying how a task is doing:
every card carries the words as well, on the chip at its foot.

THE OVERVIEW
------------
Four figures across the top. Each one is a fact the wheel below
does not already give you:

  Tasks in all        with the open / done split under it
  Overdue             and how late the oldest one is
  Due within 7 days   and when the next one falls
  With no due date    the ones that can never be flagged late

Then four panels, each answering a different question, so that
none of them repeats another:

  How the board stands  the wheel - three wedges, the total in
                        the middle, and each state's own count
                        and share read off beside it
  What needs you now    the tasks themselves, named, oldest
                        first, with how late each one is
  By priority           which priority is carrying the trouble
  By month              which month the work is piling up in

The wheel shows all three states at once. It is not a progress
meter with a single headline percentage - every state carries
its own figure, so nothing has to be judged by the size of a
wedge.

"What needs you now" lists three per group and says how many
there are in the heading, so a shortened list never hides the
size of the problem.

The four figures are always the same size as one another, and
the two panels in a row always stretch to the same height.

Every bar under "By priority" and "By month" is the same
three-band bar, worst first.

The overview always counts EVERY task. It deliberately does not
follow the filter bar underneath - that would make the numbers
move as you searched. The list below is the one that filters.

THE COLUMNS
-----------
The three priority columns share one colour at three strengths -
Priority 1 the firmest, Priority 3 the lightest. It is a cool,
quiet tint mixed from the theme's own text colour, chosen so it
does not compete with the green, amber and rose on the cards
sitting in it. The strip along the top of each column, and the
thin bar under its heading, are the page's own colour.


WEEKLY REVIEW
-------------
Nothing to fill in - it is worked out from what you have already
recorded. It gathers:

  overdue tasks, tasks due in the next 7 days, what you finished
  this week, overdue and upcoming reminders, goals marked at risk
  or on hold, people with no check-in for 14+ days, goals with no
  subtasks yet, and tasks with no due date.

"Copy this review" puts it on the clipboard as plain text, ready to
paste into an email or a message. "Print / save as PDF" gives a clean
sheet with the menu and background stripped out, for a meeting.

For "finished this week" to work, tasks need to be marked Completed
in the app - that is when the date gets recorded.


SPREADSHEETS - IMPORT AND EXPORT
--------------------------------
EVERY section has its own Import and Export buttons. You do not need
to go to Settings. Press i on any page for the same thing.

Import accepts:
  - Excel .xlsx files, straight from your computer. No conversion
    needed. If the file has several sheets you choose which one.
  - .csv and .tsv files
  - cells copied out of Excel and pasted into the box

Drag a file onto the drop area, or click to choose one.

Nothing is saved until you press the button. There are two, both
saying the same thing: one at the bottom of the window that is always
on screen, and one under the preview. It tells you what it will do -
"Save 3 rows into Budget entries".

Then, before anything is added, you get:
  - a tick box for whether the first row is column headings
  - a dropdown per field so you can correct what each column means
    (it guesses first, and is usually right)
  - a preview of the rows that will be added
  - a choice: add to what is there, or replace the whole section
  - a tick box to skip rows already in the tracker

Your sheet should be laid out the normal way: the headings across the
top row, and one record per row underneath. That is what it expects.

Headings recognised automatically include: Month, Task, Name, Title,
Status, Priority, Due date, Deadline, Date, Person, Owner, Who,
Vendor, Supplier, Paid to, By, Expense by, Amount, Budget head,
Approved by, Approver, Sanctioned by, Note, Objective, Timeline, Role.

"By" and "Approved by" are told apart correctly, so a sheet with both
columns maps the right way round.

Dates are read in any of these forms: 2026-09-05, 05/09/2026,
5-9-2026, 20-Aug-2026, and real Excel date cells. Day comes first
in the slash form.

Status words are read loosely. "done", "completed", "in progress",
"ongoing", "not started", "yet to start" are all understood.

sample-import.xlsx in this folder is a working example - two sheets,
real date cells. Try importing it to see how it behaves.

Export saves the section as a CSV that opens directly in Excel. Edit
it there and import it back if that is easier than typing.


EDITING
-------
Click almost any text and type. Editable text highlights on hover.
Press Enter to finish. Everything saves as you go.

DELETING gives you an Undo. Press the x, and a black bar appears at
the bottom with an Undo button for a few seconds. Nothing asks "are
you sure" any more except deleting a whole goal or team member, which
takes a lot of work with it.


BACKUPS - PLEASE READ
---------------------
Data is stored inside this browser, not in a file. So:
  - use the same browser every time
  - do not use a private / incognito window
  - clearing "cookies and site data" will erase everything

Settings -> Export backup saves a real .json file. Do this regularly.
Settings -> Restore backup loads it back.

If saving ever stops working, a red warning bar appears along the
bottom of the screen. If you see it, export immediately.


COLOURS
-------
style.css, at the top under :root
  --sky-1 / --sky-2 / --sky-3   the background
  --card / --ink / --line       a surface, the text on it, a border
  --accent                      the one bright colour
  --c1a / --c1b / --c1          card colour 1 (and so on up to c6)

Everything else is worked out from those in legibility.css, so a
change there moves the whole site. Each theme in themes.css sets
its own --card / --ink / --line / --accent and the rest follows.

One rule worth keeping: anything with words on it gets a solid
background. Cards, panels, banners and the menu were part-
transparent, so on some themes the scenery read straight through
the text. They are opaque now; the forest still shows in the gaps
between them, which is where it belongs.

LOADING FROM YOUR GOOGLE SHEETS
--------------------------------
The Budget page can read your ESI budget spreadsheet directly, so
you never retype an expense. Nothing about your Drive is shared
with anyone: a small script lives inside your own spreadsheet, runs
as you, and hands back only the ESI tab's rows.

Setting it up, once:

  1. Open your budget spreadsheet, then Extensions > Apps Script.
  2. Paste in the whole of budget-sync.gs (it sits in this folder,
     and is deliberately kept off GitHub because it holds the
     password on line 13).
  3. Run "checkSetup". It reports what it found and changes nothing.
  4. Deploy > New deployment > Web app.
       Execute as        : Me
       Who has access    : Anyone
     Copy the link it gives you. It ends in /exec.
  5. On the Budget page, paste that link and the password from
     line 13 into the Google Sheet box, and press
     "Connect and load".

After any edit to the script you MUST redeploy, or Google keeps
serving the old copy:
  Deploy > Manage deployments > the pencil > Version: New version
  > Deploy.
Forgetting this is the single most common reason the site says the
password is wrong.

Day to day:

  - The sheet loads by itself every time you open the site.
  - "Load from sheet now" fetches it again.
  - "Test the link" asks the script to describe itself - which
    version is live, and whether the password matched. Use it first
    whenever something looks wrong.
  - The sheet is the master copy. Every load replaces the expenses
    shown here, so make your changes in the sheet.

The link and the password are kept in this browser only. They are
never written into any file, so they cannot reach GitHub.

AND THE SAME FOR MY TASKS
-------------------------
The task list sits in a different spreadsheet, so it gets its own
script and its own web app link. The password can be the same one.

  1. Open the task spreadsheet, then Extensions > Apps Script.
  2. Paste in the whole of tasks-sync.gs (also in this folder,
     also kept off GitHub).
  3. Run "checkSetup". It lists the priority groups it found and
     the status words you actually use.
  4. Deploy > New deployment > Web app, Execute as Me, access
     Anyone. Copy the /exec link.
  5. On My Tasks, paste the link and the password into the Google
     Sheet box and press "Connect and load".

How your sheet is read:

  PO | Status | Remarks | P1 | Status | Remarks | P2 | Status | ...

Each group of three is one priority column here - the first group
becomes Priority 1, the second Priority 2, the third Priority 3.
The task name comes from the first cell, Remarks becomes the note,
and blank cells are skipped. Add a fourth group and it is picked up
by itself.

Your sheet's own status words are matched: "Done" becomes Completed,
"On track" and "Need attention" both become In progress, and a blank
cell means Not started. Because those two say more than "In progress"
does, the sheet's word is kept at the front of the note - a card
reads "Need attention - with Radha" rather than losing the warning.
"At risk", "On hold", "WIP", "Ongoing" and "Closed" are understood
too. Anything unrecognised is treated as not started.

This script only ever reads. It adds no columns and writes nothing.
Due dates are not in the sheet, so tasks arrive without one and the
month is set to the current month.

AND YOUR OWN KPIs
-----------------
Your portfolio is a third spreadsheet, so it gets a third script
and a third link. Same password again.

  1. Open your portfolio spreadsheet, then Extensions > Apps Script.
  2. Paste in the whole of kpis-sync.gs.
  3. Run "checkSetup". It lists the periods it found and how many
     KPIs carry figures.
  4. Deploy > New deployment > Web app, Execute as Me, access
     Anyone. Copy the /exec link.
  5. Go to Team Members > Narayan. The Google Sheet box sits just
     above "Key goals & KPIs". Paste the link and password there.

The box appears on your page only. Your team's KPIs come from their
portfolio workbooks in the Portfolios folder, and are untouched by
this.

How your tab is read:

  Workstream | KPI | April 2024 | May 2024 | ... | Q1 | ...

Each month or quarter heading usually spans two narrower columns,
a target and an achievement, named on the row underneath. That row
is read when it is there; without it the first column is taken as
the target. The year in the headings is ignored, so a template
still saying 2024 works unchanged.

A workstream written once and left blank down the rows below it
carries on to those rows, the way it reads on the page.

This script only ever reads. It adds no columns and writes nothing.


THEMES
------
The buttons at the top right switch the whole world. There are
twelve: Dark, Digital, Forest, Racing, City, Minimalist, Home,
Travel, Village, Military, Artistic and Indian music. One at a
time, and the choice is remembered on this computer.

To change how a theme looks, open themes.css and find its block
(they are numbered 1 to 12). The colours at the top of each block
are the whole UI — cards, text, lines, the accent. Everything
below them is that theme's scenery.

To change what a theme draws, open themes.js and find its entry
in SCENES. Each one returns two pieces of HTML: bg goes behind
the pages, fg goes in front of them.

To add a theme, add one line to the THEMES list in themes.js, add
a matching function to SCENES, and add a colour block to
themes.css. Nothing else needs touching.

Forest is the original world. Choosing it puts back the trees,
the animals and the old weather.
